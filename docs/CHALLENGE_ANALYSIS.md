# NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 - Challenge Analysis

## Challenge: Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map

**Source URL:** https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/interplanetary-survival-guide-martian-map/
**Accessed:** 2026-11-15
**Event:** 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Exact Description

NASA has explored Mars robotically for decades, studying its extreme environment, mapping its surface, and collecting many types of data. Far from home, the first astronauts to set foot on Mars will want the best map possible, with information on the details of their routes and destinations, updates on current conditions, and the data needed to complete their mission quickly and safely. Your challenge is to create a layered, integrated view of a location or route on the Martian surface that pulls together data from multiple NASA science missions and could help a human explorer plan and carry out a successful Marswalk while conducting new and exciting science along the way.

## Objectives

1. Create a map, tool, or interface a Mars astronaut could use during a surface exploration mission
2. Use NASA data and tools
3. Identify scientific goals for the mission and the location the astronaut will explore
4. Include at least three kinds of data layered or combined (optical, thermal, spectral, topographic, geophysical, atmospheric, radiation, chemical)
5. Prioritize astronaut safety - include information to help crew move safely
6. Consider radiation environment, space weather data
7. Consider mobility constraints (spacesuits, equipment, supplies)
8. Include route map showing travel to accomplish scientific objectives while avoiding obstacles

## Intended Users

- Primary: Mars astronauts during surface exploration missions (EVAs/Marswalks)
- Secondary: Mission planners, scientists, flight controllers
- Tertiary: Public outreach/education

## Expected Outputs

From the challenge description and submission guidance, expected outputs include:
- Interactive digital map/tool/interface (preferred)
- Static snapshot acceptable but less competitive
- Heads-up display (HUD) concept for spacesuit visor
- Tablet application for crew carry
- Spacecraft/habitat planning display
- Route maps with waypoints
- Layered data visualizations (3+ data types)
- Point-of-view imagery integration

## Suggested Data and Resources (from Resources tab)

1. **JPL Mars Trek** - Web-based portal for Mars exploration, showcases data from various landing sites
2. **USGS-NASA Planetary Geologic Mapping Program** - High-quality standardized geologic maps of Mars
3. **Curiosity rover route example** - Annotated proposed route for Curiosity climbing Mount Sharp
4. **NASA Worldview** - Satellite imagery exploration (Earth-focused but methodology applicable)
5. **Mars Perseverance Raw Images Gallery** - 1M+ images from rover journey

## Key Constraints & Requirements

- **Must use real NASA data** - No fabricated data
- **At least 3 data types** layered/combined
- **Astronaut safety priority** - Route planning, hazard avoidance
- **Hyperlocal focus** - Individual EVA scale, not just global/regional
- **Honest documentation** - Distinguish integrated vs. investigated data
- **License compliance** - All code MIT or compatible
- **Coordinate system correctness** - Mars planetocentric/planetographic
